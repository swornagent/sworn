## W0 admission

### Verdict

The new namespace is pristine and admissible after publication and protected
approval, subject to one mandatory runner correction.

### Mandatory correction

The old temporary maintenance-bridge runner used a
`resolveBehavioralInertness` callback that unconditionally returned `inert`.
That callback is trusted host policy and must not be reused.

The RC3 admission runner may return `inert` only when both conditions are
proved:

1. the exact commit has the same non-record product identity as the certified
   bridge/product identity; and
2. the transition is structurally confined to `.baton/releases/**`.

Any other commit, path, product tree, missing evidence, or ambiguous
classification stops before a ref moves.

### Safe admission sequence

1. Reconstruct the annotated public tag, peel, tree, unique release asset,
   archive commit, archive safety, generated adapters, and package digest in
   fresh isolated copies.
2. Fill every final Plan identity, parse the exact raw bytes with RC3, and
   obtain one exact protected owner approval.
3. Reconfirm the clean target identity, pristine new namespace, and unchanged
   archaeology immediately before installation.
4. Resolve only the exact approval evidence and the exact behavioral-inertness
   claim above.
5. Install once, replay once, and require one release ref, no owner refs,
   unchanged target, exact installed Plan bytes, and an idempotent second
   receipt.
6. Materialize only `T0-admission`, replay it idempotently, then create
   worktrees from the already-created refs.
7. Write a fresh design, obtain Captain `PROCEED`, replay only the W0 product
   delta, regenerate RC3 assets, and produce fresh proof and verification.
8. Materialize T1 and T2 only after W0 has passed and composed.

The old W0 commit must not be cherry-picked. The audited old W0 product delta
is replay input only:

## W1 authority core

### Verdict

The authority architecture is reusable, but the old candidate has a real RC3
semantic gap and cannot be replayed unchanged.

Reusable foundations include:

- symbolic-ref batch capture;
- the absolute, sanitized literal Git boundary;
- prepared `update-ref --no-deref --stdin` transactions;
- commit-object and OID validation;
- prepared-lock alias refusal;
- strict records and lifecycle actions;
- exact product composition; and
- retry reconstruction above the repository boundary.

Required repairs:

1. Probe a requested ref omitted from `for-each-ref` with both bounded
   `symbolic-ref` and `show-ref --verify --quiet`. Symbolic-ref failure alone
   cannot distinguish genuine absence from a broken direct ref.
2. Bind every atomic update to exact pre-state, desired-state, and meaningful
   ref vectors.
3. After every helper, transport, protocol, timeout, signal, exit, or output
   failure, capture fresh exact state and classify:
   - all desired: success;
   - all meaningful refs at pre-state: typed snapshot-scoped not-applied;
   - mixed, third, symbolic, broken, non-commit, or failed reconciliation:
     typed recovery-required ambiguity.
4. Bound the transaction to 10 seconds and stdout/stderr to 512 KiB. Always
   kill, wait, and prove lock cleanup.
5. Carry typed outcomes through `internal/baton`; no caller may parse error
   prose.
6. Describe an all-pre result as “currently at captured pre-state.” It cannot
   prove that no commit-and-revert ABA occurred.

Fresh evidence must cover SHA-1 and SHA-256 repositories, genuine absence,
broken direct refs, resolving and dangling aliases, create/update/verify races,
pre-commit faults, post-commit faults, every reconciliation class, output
overflow, lock cleanup, ABA, multi-ref actions, and crash-before-receipt replay.

Relevant implementation surfaces are `internal/gitx/refs.go` and
`internal/baton/repository.go`. Baton’s binding oracle is
`reference/records/git.mjs` and its high-risk corpus is
`test/records/git-trust-adversarial.test.mjs`.

## W2 driver core

### Verdict

The role-neutral driver core is strongly reusable, but isolation and bounding
repairs are required before fresh RC3 proof.

The old product candidate and later old-lineage head have byte-identical
`internal/driver` trees; their only differences are old proof and status
records. Reusable foundations include:

- the strict `baton.driver/v1` codec and operation binding;
- explicit Planner, Implementer, Captain, and Verifier selection;
- deterministic Merge refusal from production dispatch;
- responsibility-shaped, one-shot sealed submissions;
- ordered digest-bound inputs;
- pinned fresh processes with empty environments;
- process-group timeout and cancellation;
- transcript reduction;
- reported, unavailable, and legitimate-zero usage semantics; and
- the deterministic fake and shared conformance harness.

Required repairs:

1. Do not mount the complete caller workspace at its original host path.
   Baton records, journal, approval resolver, engine logs, and canonical
   worktree paths must be absent from the model-visible filesystem.
2. Do not create transient `.sworn-inputs` inside the underlying workspace.
   Input projection must be constructed outside the source worktree before
   dispatch, including for clean read-only verification.
3. Require Verifier dispatch to be both `read_only` and
   `fresh_context=true`, not merely role/responsibility-shaped.
4. Give stderr and every other output a production maximum and terminate on
   overflow; truncation alone is not a bound.
5. Add an adversarial parent-cancellation and timeout case after an accepted
   submission and prove that no handoff escapes.
6. Rebind names, fixtures, and goldens to the published RC3 assets even where
   canonical operation and schema bytes remain unchanged.

Fresh evidence must plant forbidden-surface canaries, detect transient source
mutation, reject writable or nonfresh Verifiers, flood stderr, cancel after
submission, exercise replay/conflict behavior, distinguish usage states, and
retain deterministic Merge refusal.

Relevant implementation surfaces are `internal/driver/process_linux.go`,
`projection.go`, `submission.go`, `selection.go`, `invoke.go`, `usage.go`, and
the shared conformance/process fixtures.

## W3 walking skeleton

### Verdict

W3 is greenfield runtime work over repaired W1 and W2 seams. The post-reset
`internal/journal` and `internal/runtime` packages are ownership placeholders,
`cmd/sworn` exposes help/version only, and `test/e2e` does not exist.

Do not replay the archived engine reducer, delivery/work phases, controller
services, mutable submission/verdict tables, provider-specific configuration,
or nine-migration store. They duplicate lifecycle now owned by Baton. The old
store also used ordinary `BeginTx`, not the required `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`
transaction model.

Useful patterns and test ideas include:

- a private exact database identity and one serialized SQLite connection;
- read-only opening that cannot create or migrate;
- immutable same-ID/same-bytes replay and conflicting-byte refusal;
- finite generation/token claims;
- claim transaction, external execution, then completion/reconciliation
  transaction;
- corruption, locking, path-swap, crash, and restart fixtures; and
- typed performed, retryable, and uncertain effect outcomes.

Required package boundaries are:

- `internal/journal`: SQLite schema plus canonical
  command/effect/claim/receipt/event facts, never mutable Baton lifecycle;
- `internal/runtime`: derive from current Baton authority, resolve external
  approval, run roles outside transactions, reconcile effects, and invoke
  Baton actions;
- `internal/gitx`: dependency-ordered typed APIs for disposable worktrees,
  exact identity, clean read-only verification worktrees, and sealed product
  import—never arbitrary caller Git;
- `cmd/sworn`: trusted configuration and dependency wiring only; and
- `test/e2e`: disposable-repository walking skeleton and crash matrix.

W3 must expose the read-only seams W6 later consumes: immutable journal
snapshot plus event offset, bounded opaque evidence/log references, multi-run
metadata, replay-stable typed commands, and a best-effort typed observer with a
no-op default. The observer receives redacted facts after durable decisions,
cannot block or retry delivery, and does not turn the journal into an OTLP
queue.

Fresh tests must cover exact approval provenance and non-self-approval,
idempotent Plan installation, full fake Planner-through-integration flow,
separate clean work and assembly Verifiers, same/conflicting command replay,
one-winner claims, stale completion rejection, crash cuts around every
external effect, target movement after assembly PASS, database identity and
corruption, read-only behavior, and telemetry/outbox non-interference.

W3 adds one pinned direct dependency, `modernc.org/sqlite`, preserving
`CGO_ENABLED=0`. Its transitive and binary cost must be measured immediately
with twin VCS-free builds.

## W4 topology and recovery

### Verdict

W4 is greenfield scheduling and recovery over W3. It must consume W1's exact
Snapshot and mechanical Baton actions, not create another oracle or lifecycle.

The original Coach behavior and current Baton authority agree on ownership:
select the current structurally authoritative record proved by owner
materialization or exact composition transfer. Never select a generic
“furthest” record by timestamp, lifecycle rank, completion order, or
later-looking divergent state.

Topology invariants are:

- `release-wt` owns assembly and serial composition;
- one durable claim owns each active track writer;
- separate tracks may run concurrently when plan dependencies are satisfied;
- work remains serial within a track;
- scheduling and composition use plan DAG plus plan order;
- a parked track gates its dependants and assembly but not an independent
  ready track; and
- canonical authority worktrees remain separate from model-facing product
  projections.

Recovery invariants are:

- transport, unchanged-responsibility, and unchanged-candidate/status retry
  budgets remain exactly `3 / 3 / 2`;
- restart, timer, log, lease expiry, takeover, transport error, and identical
  command replay never count as progress;
- only new authoritative Baton state, a new candidate, or a successfully
  reconciled external-effect receipt is progress;
- lease expiry does not authorize takeover until the prior process tree is
  quiescent and its effect is reconciled;
- pause stops new dispatch, cancel terminates active process trees, retry is
  typed and idempotent, and takeover changes runtime ownership only;
- exhaustion remains operational and never synthesizes a Baton verdict; and
- post-materialization replan preserves old lineage and uses newly approved
  release/work identities.

The minimum deterministic corpus includes a parked/independent/dependant
three-track DAG, two workers racing one track, restart-twice cuts around every
effect and database bind, exact persisted counters, distinct exhaustion
routing, invalid/divergent authority, finish-order-independent composition,
composition conflict, moved target after assembly PASS, assembly
FAIL/BLOCKED, cancel-after-submit, cancel-during-CAS, and immutable-lineage
replan.

W4 should add no direct dependency beyond W3 SQLite. A reasonable early
estimate is 2,000–3,000 production lines plus a comparably serious adversarial
corpus; this must be remeasured rather than hidden behind scheduler helpers.

## W5 production adapters

### Verdict

There is no W5 implementation to replay. The old T4 head only materialized the
track over composed W2 and left W5 at `design / ready / implementer`. It has no
design, Captain decision, product candidate, proof, or verdict. Its product
tree is unchanged from the W2 lineage.

W5 must therefore be designed and implemented fresh after the repaired W2
passes and composes. Older Codex and credential code is archaeology from the
retired engine, not replay input.

### Shared infrastructure

W5 stays within the common role-neutral driver and should add:

- one certifier and canonical runtime-closure manifest;
- one credential-handle and private-generation boundary;
- one contained native-process launcher shared by Codex and Claude;
- one bounded HTTP tool loop shared by all HTTP profiles;
- one OpenAI-compatible codec shared by OpenAI-compatible endpoints and the
  named DeepSeek profile;
- small Gemini and Bedrock codecs; and
- canonical `inspect`, `doctor`, and `certify` results.

The current W2 runtime mounts broad host `/usr`, library, and certificate
surfaces. W5 cannot certify that boundary. Each profile needs an exact declared
closure covering the executable, libraries, toolchain, CA bundle, DNS, hosts,
NSS inputs, and permitted workspace tools. Drift, an undeclared mount/tool, or
unusable DNS/NSS/CA input stops before inference.

`CredentialRef` must become an opaque configured handle plus private
credential generation bound into the sealed permission descriptor. It must not
serialize, log, stringify, or expose credential bytes or cleartext
identifiers. Local generation substitution is detectable, while provider
account identity is claimed only when an authenticated provider response
asserts it.

Capability certification must prove the configured profile/model supports all
requested tool, function-call, structured-result, system-message, usage, and
cancellation behavior before opening a process or request. Unknown or
default-on capability is failure, not assumed support.

### Native profiles

Codex and Claude share the runner boundary but keep their exact vendor-native
agent loops. Each profile must bind the final resolved native ELF, version,
digest, runtime closure, closed feature/tool surface, unattended arguments,
structured event/result parser, cancellation semantics, and credential
visibility proof.

The retired Codex code contains useful adversarial concepts—static ELF
validation, locked single-link credential refresh, descriptor-bound private
probes, identity-swap refusal, and child-process secret canaries. It is coupled
to the removed executor and role-specific adapters, pins a stale CLI, and uses
old bypass assumptions. Those concepts may inform fresh tests; the code must
not be transplanted wholesale.

Final arguments and feature closures are derived from the pinned binaries
under test. User configuration, rules, memories, session persistence, MCP,
plugins, hooks, browsers, multi-agent features, ambient skills, and every
other undeclared extension surface remain disabled or absent.

### HTTP and cloud profiles

The shared loop—not provider codecs—owns the contained workspace shell,
`sworn_submit`, turn/tool/body/output/time limits, cancellation, and terminal
submission behavior. Any attempted `sworn_submit`, accepted or rejected,
terminates the invocation and permits no later provider or tool turn.

All HTTP profiles reject redirects, implicit transport retries, streaming,
SSE, eventstream, unexpected content types, oversized bodies, and compressed
expansion beyond the configured bound.

Profile-specific requirements include:

- OpenAI-compatible base URLs preserve an explicit path prefix, reject
  userinfo/fragments, require HTTPS off loopback, and allow loopback HTTP only
  in marked fixtures.
- DeepSeek remains configuration over the OpenAI-compatible codec.
  `reasoning_content` may continue a provider exchange but never becomes tool
  arguments, submitted artifacts, or final handoff text.
- Gemini deterministically correlates `functionCall` and `functionResponse`
  parts and handles safety blocks, finish states, malformed candidates, and
  usage explicitly.
- `bedrock_explicit_v1` accepts only an injected explicit credential resolver
  and explicit region. It never falls back to environment, shared profiles,
  process credentials, web identity, ECS, or IMDS.
- Bedrock fixtures bind service, region, date, optional session token, signed
  headers, payload hash, canonical request, and exactly-once path escaping for
  model IDs and inference-profile ARNs.

The standard library is sufficient for HTTP, JSON, HMAC, SHA-256, and SigV4.
Provider SDKs would add dependency and binary weight without owning any Baton
or orchestration responsibility.

## W6 cockpit, evaluation, and OpenTelemetry

### Verdict

Do not replay the v0.2 board, TUI, telemetry, or evaluation product. Re-derive
only the RC3 oracle semantics, generation-guarded refresh/last-good UX
patterns, and privacy/non-interference test ideas.

The old board reads obsolete documentation files, falls back to HEAD and the
working tree, and elects by timestamps. The old TUI adds settings, API-key
editing, agent launch/defer/gate actions, raw filesystem logs, and a large
Bubble Tea/Lipgloss stack. The old telemetry uses custom HTTP, one unbounded
goroutine per event, silent errors, a persistent install identifier, and
evaluation that skips unreadable rows and confuses zero with missing. All are
incompatible with W6.

W6 must consume interfaces created by prior work:

- W1 supplies the single exact captured-ref authority projection;
- W3 supplies immutable snapshot plus offset, evidence/log reads, multi-run
  metadata, typed command receipts, and the observer interface; and
- W4 supplies the closed pause/resume/cancel/retry/takeover service.

Git and SQLite cannot share one transaction. The cockpit displays their exact
snapshot identities separately and never claims cross-store atomicity.

`internal/cockpit` should use standard-library HTTP and one embedded
HTML/CSS/JS file. It accepts opaque IDs and typed commands only—never a shell,
path, ref, Git command, or direct Baton write. Both terminal JSON and WebUI
consume one oracle rather than rebuilding authority.

`internal/observe` owns deterministic evaluation from one pinned journal
snapshot and direct OTLP/HTTP OpenTelemetry. Runtime and journal remain
OTel-free. No Bubble Tea, browser framework, vendor telemetry SDK, embedded
Collector, or OTel logs are required.

The cockpit corpus must cover desktop/mobile browsers, keyboard-only use,
focus and last-good retention, semantic accessibility, reduced motion,
Host/Origin/auth rejection, credential non-disclosure, command replay and
conflict, stale revisions, uncertain effects, snapshot offset gaps,
out-of-order/duplicate events, removed/corrupt runs, stale async refresh,
opaque evidence/log drilldown, path/symlink/oversize refusal, and zero direct
Baton writes.

Evaluation must emit byte-identical stable-order canonical JSON, retain
numerator/denominator/unknown on every aggregate, distinguish observed zero
from unavailable, refuse changed snapshots and duplicate/corrupt rows, use
immutable expected labels only, and independently derive exact integration
from Baton identities.

OTel is disabled by default and must survive slow, hung, rejecting, partial,
full-queue, restarting, and unavailable exporters without changing any
candidate, verdict, receipt, target, or exit status. A central typed allowlist
rejects unknown fields and every prompt, completion, source, diff, path,
credential, argv, arbitrary error, finding prose, tool payload, and raw log.
Metrics use finite-enum labels only; model/provider/IDs/versions/cases/attempts
never become labels.

A reasonable early W6 target is 1,600–2,200 runtime Go lines plus the embedded
asset and tests. Direct OTLP/HTTP OTel commonly adds four modules; combined
with SQLite this likely consumes five of the final eight direct-dependency
slots. Measure module and stripped-binary deltas as soon as the T3 baseline
exists.
